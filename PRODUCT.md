# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

Perth homeowners on scheme (mains) water. They are not chasing filtration as a topic — they have a specific domestic irritation: the water tastes or smells of chlorine, the kettle furs up, the shower screen scales, the glassware comes out cloudy. Technical literacy is low and interest in the chemistry is close to zero. They want the annoyance gone and they want to trust whoever installs it.

Secondary, and only where it does not distort the primary: households on bore or tank supply with harder problems. Not the audience the site is designed around.

## Product Purpose

Sell and install a whole-home water filtration system across the Perth metro. Next Gen is the product brand; Aqua-Safe Plumbing & Maintenance are the licensed plumbers who install it. That split is deliberate — the product carries the premium positioning, and Aaron's existing trade credibility and real Google reviews carry the trust.

Success is a booked in-home water test. The test is the product's own best salesperson: it turns an abstract worry about water into a specific result on this house's kitchen bench.

## Positioning

The engineering is the reason it works, not the reason to buy. Everyone else in this category sells either fear or wellness; this sells a household annoyance quietly and completely solved, with the machinery available underneath for anyone who wants to look.

## Conversion & proof

- Primary CTA: book a free in-home water test.
- Secondary CTA: not yet decided. Do not invent one — a weak fallback dilutes the primary.
- The line a visitor remembers after 10 seconds: not yet written. It should be an outcome, not a specification.
- Belief ladder: not yet confirmed with the client. Working hypothesis to validate, not to build on — (1) my water actually has something in it worth removing, (2) this fixes it at every tap rather than one jug, (3) the people installing it are competent and licensed, (4) booking a test costs me nothing.
- Proof on hand: manufacturer certification documents (NSF / WaterMark — not yet sighted); photographs of real completed installs; Aqua-Safe's genuine Google reviews, usable as *installer* credibility and never as product performance.

⚠️ **Reduction percentages are not proof.** `src/content/booking.ts` carries up-to-90/95/96/98/99% figures that the page itself labels placeholders pending NATA-accredited testing. Unsubstantiated reduction claims about drinking water are misleading conduct under the Australian Consumer Law. They either become attributed manufacturer claims backed by sighted certification, or they come off and the mechanism is described instead. See `src/lib/seo.ts`.

## Brand Personality

Premium restraint. Closer to a high-end appliance or audio brand than to a plumbing product — spare, expensive-feeling, unhurried, and entirely unbothered about convincing you. Confidence shown by how little it needs to say.

## Anti-references

Wellness and health-store aesthetics. No sage or blush, no rounded friendly type, no "pure living" language, no leaves or water droplets as decoration. This is the nearest and most dangerous drift for anything touching what people put in their bodies, and it is the one the client explicitly rejected.

## Design Principles

**Lead with the outcome, prove with the engineering.** The homeowner meets the result first. The machine, the media, the tolerances are all still there, one level down, doing the work of proof rather than the work of persuasion. This inverts what the site currently does.

**Restraint is the premium signal.** Every element removed makes the remaining ones more expensive-looking. Where there is a choice between showing more and showing less, show less and make it better.

**Never claim what cannot be substantiated.** A number without a source is a liability, not a selling point. Describe what the machine does and how, which is true, verifiable and more interesting than a percentage.

**The installer is part of the product.** Licensed plumbers, real reviews, actual completed jobs. The trust does not come from the website; it comes from Aaron, and the site's job is to make that visible.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA as the working standard — not yet confirmed with the client, so treat as default rather than requirement. The site runs heavy 3D and scroll-driven motion, so a genuine `prefers-reduced-motion` path is a hard requirement, not a nicety, and contrast must be verified against the dark palette rather than assumed.
